# TREATMENT — CapsEasy promo, 1930s Rubber Hose Cartoon style

**Film:** `capseasy-rubber` · Batch 4 style test · 1080×1920 · 24 fps · 30.0 s · no voiceover
**Style:** 1930s Rubber Hose Cartoon — `styles/rubber-hose/STYLE.md` (bouncy big-band cartoon)
**Director workflow:** lemo-opuscar (`AGENTS.md` → `DIRECTOR.md` → `TECHNIQUE.md` → `STYLE.md` → treatment → style frames → cue map → build → review → QC).
No LemoLab end card, no watermark (repo-demo artifacts only).

## 1. Logline and arc

**Logline:** Milo, a rubber-hose cartoon creator, is hunted by his own tools —
runaway timecodes, a scolding clock, giant scissors — until a friendly lavender
machine does the captioning for him, and the whole set dances him into posting.

**Arc (setup → struggle → triumph → CTA):**
- **Setup (bars 1–2):** Title card "CAPTION CHAOS!" rolls up like a window
  shade. Milo at his desk, buried under timecode slips, typing with a pencil nub.
- **Struggle (bars 3–7):** The slips wake and scatter (chase, slips = xylophone
  eighths). Milo's arm stretches to grab one. The grumpy clock wakes, scolds
  "TICK-TOCK!". Giant scissors wake, snip-snip — the chase reverses. Milo ends
  wrapped in timecode tape like a mummy.
- **Breath (bar 8):** Band stops dead. Projector clatter only. Milo looks up.
  One lavender "ding" — the CapsEasy machine rolls in. Lock eyes.
- **Triumph (bars 9–15):** Slot whoosh, clip in. Whisper puffs (zigzag →
  straight-up alarm). "your Groq key" tag pops. 13 template tiles hop like piano
  keys (ascending xylo). Milo picks tile #7 — lavender glow. Machine cranks, an
  MP4 pops out with real captions. Key change up: the whole world dances —
  scissors snip party ribbons, the clock smiles, slips bounce as a chorus line.
  Ta-da freeze.
- **CTA (bars 16–18):** Iris closes on the dancers, opens on the punchline card:
  "stop timing captions. / start posting." Final brass hit; Milo's glove pulls
  the iris shut. The film ends on the card's promise.

## 2. Benchmark

1. **Fleischer Studios shorts c.1930–33** (named in STYLE.md §references).
   *Learn:* the whole world breathes on the beat; objects as living cast members;
   iris and shade-rolled title cards as scene grammar.
   *Do NOT take:* any character design, gag, or shot.
2. **Cuphead** (named in STYLE.md §references).
   *Learn:* how a modern team finishes the rubber-hose look (constant-width ink,
   hard form shadows, stepped acting with smooth camera) and scores it hot.
   *Do NOT take:* characters, bosses, levels, or music.
3. **Original score written to the grid** (style-native: tuba oom-pah, banjo 2&4,
   trumpet stabs, clarinet, xylophone, slide whistle — no string pads).

**Standing hard constraints:**
- **No AI slop.** No generic gradients, no floating particles/bokeh, no filler
  copy, no stock clichés. Painted-cream proscenium, hand-inked cast, nothing else.
- **Sharper content.** Every line is short, punchy, human, built only on verified
  CapsEasy facts (see §8). No filler beats; every second advances
  setup → struggle → triumph → CTA.
- **Zero overlapping.** Strict layout discipline: no text over text, no graphics
  covering key action; critical content stays in the 9:16 safe frame
  (clear of the top notch ~180 px and bottom home-indicator ~120 px; the bottom
  12% is reserved for subtitles). Collision pass on frame sheets before delivery.
- **Characters original.** No existing cartoon character, name, or silhouette;
  the hero is an original creator character, not the demo's mug or cube.

### Tempo map — how pacing is shaped across the 30 s

Setup → struggle → breath → triumph → held CTA, with deliberately varied rhythm:

- **0–3.33 (bars 1–2) — SETUP, medium pulse.** Title vamp at 144 with the theme
  stated plainly. Letters land on beats 1–5 with space between hops — the film
  announces itself, then the shade-roll gives a 0.8 s glide before the set.
- **3.33–11.67 (bars 3–7) — STRUGGLE, quickening.** Same 144 grid, but rhythmic
  *density* ramps: bar 3 = half-time feel (typing), bar 4 adds clarinet runs,
  bars 5–6 add tom fills and trombone stabs, bar 7 is full agitato (every eighth
  filled). The picture quickens with it: locked wide → trucking pan → close-up →
  reversed pan. No flat fast tempo — the *escalation* is the engine.
- **11.67–13.33 (bar 8) — BREATH, near-stop.** Band chokes to true silence
  (projector clatter only) for a full beat, then one soft clarinet phrase.
  Picture freezes too (background breathing included) — the longest-held acting
  in the film. The single "ding" at 12.5 is the first sound after the silence
  and the most important sound in the film.
- **13.33–21.67 (bars 9–13) — TRIUMPH, accelerating.** Band returns brushed and
  gentle (the machine is kind), then *accelerates*: slot → whisper → tiles
  (the xylo run is the fastest figure in the film) → pick → crank → pop. Each
  bar hands off with a J-cut (next scene's sound arrives early).
- **21.67–24.58 (bars 14–15) — PEAK, then freeze.** Key change up; the loudest,
  densest 2 bars (everyone dances, breathing ×3). Then a hard freeze: ta-da
  pose held 1.4 s while the band holds its final chord.
- **24.58–30.0 (bars 16–18) — HELD CTA, slow close.** Iris wipes the frenzy
  away; the punchline card sits for 4.4 s of near-stillness (only the sunburst
  creeps and the bass pedals). Final brass hit at 29.17, then the iris gag
  closes the film at 30.0. Silence is the punctuation.

## 8. Verified CapsEasy facts used (and only these)

Free, open-source, local-first AI video captioning. Upload a talking-head clip
→ Whisper transcription via the user's own Groq key (BYOK) → 13 cinematic
caption templates → 1080×1920 MP4 rendered locally on the user's machine.
Footage never leaves the machine ("footage stays on your machine"). The machine's marquee
scrolls "FREE • OPEN-SOURCE • LOCAL-FIRST". No metrics, no users, no revenue,
no claims beyond these.

## 9. Palette (campaign-locked, style-adapted)

Cream-dominant: paper `#ffffeb` major surface, washes `#f5f2d8` / `#efeccf` for
walls/floor (tints of cream, never gray). Ink `#1a1a1a` — all linework,
characters own the extremes (character whites `#ffffff`, ink `#1a1a1a`).
Accents only: lavender `#f0d7ff` (CapsEasy machine body details, hero tile glow,
"ding" star, CTA highlight) and deep green `#034f46` (clock face details, MP4
screen border, film-leader ticks, CTA sub-line). Form shadows = ink at low alpha
(hard-edged bands). No other colors anywhere.

## 10. Motion rules (from the brief + STYLE.md §4)

Characters on twos (12 fps stepping via `q(t)`); camera on ones (24 fps).
144 BPM → 1 beat = 10 frames; every beat lands on an even frame so twos hit it.
Breathing: `cos(2π·beats)` — stretch on the beat, squash on the off-beat,
amplitude ~6%, all props in phase; ×3 at the dance climax (plus the desk set
squashes ~2.5% around the desk line). Anticipation–hold–action on every big move
(crouch to 0.8, hold while the band stops, snap). Easing on every move; one
world→screen camera function; designed transitions only (shade roll, iris;
no unmotivated hard cuts — the one hard cut is the clock close-up, motivated
by the alarm). Line boil ±1.2 px cycling 3 drawings at 12 fps; backgrounds never
boil. Film damage in the composite pass: gate weave ±1 px, flicker ±2.5%,
faint vignette, rare faint scratch — light enough for the cream palette
(grain goes in ffmpeg, level 1).
