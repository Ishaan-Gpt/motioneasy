# TREATMENT — "CAPTIONS" (a photoplay in one reel)

**CapsEasy, 1920s Silent Film style · 1080×1920 · 30.0 s · 24 fps · no dialogue, no voiceover**

## 1. Logline and arc

A film about captions told entirely in intertitles. Setup: in 1926 a title-letterer
hand-paints every card, frame by frame. Turn: a century later a creator does the same
drudgery digitally — scrubbing timelines at 2 a.m., hand-timing every word. Ending: the
talkie moment — one click, and CapsEasy gives the film its voice: captions appear,
timed, styled, in seconds. End card: "stop timing captions. start posting."

One subject (the caption), one goal (getting words on screen), one turn (the machine
finally does it). Ending echoes the opening: the hand that lettered cards is replaced
by the hand that clicks once.

## 2. Benchmark

- **The Artist (2011)** — how few intertitles a modern audience needs; hold each card
  long enough to read AND feel. Learn: restraint, held cards, the talkie-arrives gag.
  Don't take: its story or its ending.
- **Buster Keaton, Sherlock Jr. (1924)** — deadpan performance, locked wide shots, the
  gag in one frame. Learn: staging clarity, projection-speed changes as emotion.
  Don't take: any gag or look.
- **1920s photoplay piano cue sheets** — music by section (Maestoso / Andante / Hurry /
  Misterioso / silence / resolve). Learn: the piano IS the sound design; silence as a
  section. Don't take: any published melody — the theme is original.

## 7. Subtitle and title design

The intertitles ARE the subtitles; the .srt lists their texts. Type: Playfair Display
SC (Black/Bold) for shouts and the title, Old Standard TT Italic for narration —
both OFL, self-hosted. Cards are cream (#ffffeb) with ink (#1a1a1a) text and deco
borders (level 0 = heavy rule for the shout; level 1 = double rule + fans; level 2 =
vines for the tender/silence card); deep-green (#034f46) diamond accents and lavender
(#f0d7ff) caption pills are the only hues, kept via the print's colour mask.

Cards (text / hold):
1. CAPTIONS / *a photoplay in one reel* — 2.0–6.0
2. *every word on screen.* / *by hand.* — 9.0–12.0
3. *a century later…* — 12.0–14.0
4. **drag. sync. repeat.** — 16.0–18.0
5. *then the film found its voice.* — 18.0–21.2
6. (in-scene captions) *the film finally found its voice.* — 21.5–24.4
7. *timed. styled. in seconds.* → **stop timing captions.** / **start posting.** /
   *capseasy · free & open-source · local-first* — 24.4–30.0

## 8. Tempo map — how the 30 seconds breathe

Setup (0–8.8): slow and stately — Maestoso title, Andante letterer. Struggle
(9–18): the quicken — cards arrive faster, the piano accelerates into the Hurry,
the montage flickers harder. Triumph (21.2–30): the SILENCE (18–21.2) is the hinge —
3.2 s of near-nothing so the CLICK and the bright resolve feel enormous — then the
end card is built slowly in three passes and held. A flat fast tempo is banned:
the film must feel like 1926 → 2 a.m. → the talkie arriving.
